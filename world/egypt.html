Egypt · 2560 BCE

The Great Pyramid of Giza is completed

The Great Pyramid is built for Pharaoh Khufu at Giza around 2560 BCE. It remains the tallest human-made structure for roughly 3,800 years and is the only surviving Wonder of the Ancient World.

Somalia · 2500 BCE

The Land of Punt

Egyptian records describe Punt, a land of incense, gold and ebony reached by sea down the Red Sea coast; many scholars place it on the Horn of Africa near present-day Somalia. Trade between Punt and Egypt runs for centuries, carrying frankincense and myrrh north to the Nile.

Pakistan · 2600 BCE

Mohenjo-daro

Mohenjo-daro is one of the largest cities of the Indus civilization, with planned streets, standardized brickwork, drainage and large public structures. Its political and religious organization remains difficult to reconstruct because the Indus script is undeciphered and no unambiguous royal palace has been identified.

Central African Republic · 2500 BCE

Bouar

On the plateaux near Bouar, people raise standing stones up to four meters high and set them over burial pits. Hundreds of these tazunu monuments dot the western hills, the oldest raised long before writing reaches the region. Who lifts them, and why, is not recorded; only the stones remain, leaning in the grass.

Cyprus · 2500 BCE

The copper that names the island

Miners work the rich copper ores of the Troodos foothills, and the metal grows so tied to the island that its Latin name echoes that of Cyprus itself. The mine at Skouriotissa is worked, on and off, for thousands of years.

Eritrea · 2500 BCE

The Gash Group

Archaeological sites of the Gash Group in Eritrea's western lowlands preserve settled communities of farmers and herders with distinctive pottery and long-distance exchange. Imported materials show connections with the Nile Valley and Red Sea worlds.

Niger · 2500 BCE

Pearl millet feeds the Sahel

Across the grasslands south of the desert, farmers cultivate pearl millet, a drought-hardy grain domesticated in the West African Sahel. Millet becomes the staple that carries villages through the long dry season.
