Brazil · 1831

Pedro I abdicates

Emperor Pedro I abdicates the Brazilian throne in 1831 amid political conflict and returns to Europe. His five-year-old son Pedro II remains emperor under a regency until he is declared of age in 1840.

Meanwhile, elsewhere…

Grenada · 1843

Nutmeg makes the Isle of Spice

Nutmeg trees, carried from the East Indies, take to Grenada's volcanic soil. Within a lifetime the island becomes a major global nutmeg producer, the fruit later stamped on its flag. Split nutmeg and its red lace of mace still dry in the Gouyave sheds, the reason Grenada is called the Isle of Spice.

Algeria · 1845

French conquest of Algeria

During the French conquest, troops under Colonel Aimable Pélissier seal members of the Ouled Riah inside caves in the Dahra and kill hundreds by smoke and fire. The wider conquest brings warfare, displacement, famine and disease and causes severe population loss.

Barbados · 1831

1831 Great Barbados hurricane

A ferocious storm tears across Barbados, unroofing nearly every building in Bridgetown, snapping the masts of ships in the Careenage, and killing roughly fifteen hundred people. It ruins the sugar crop and leaves the island rebuilding on the eve of emancipation.

Bosnia and Herzegovina · 1831

The Dragon of Bosnia

Captain Husein Gradaščević leads Bosnia's lords in revolt against the sultan's centralising reforms, demanding autonomy for the province. His army beats an Ottoman force in the field before rival Bosnian nobles and imperial troops break the rising. The 'Dragon of Bosnia' dies in exile at thirty.

And far away…

Guyana · 1831

British Guiana

In 1831 Britain combines the colonies of Essequibo, Demerara and Berbice into British Guiana. Georgetown becomes the capital, while Dutch legal, hydraulic and place-name legacies remain important under British rule.

Elsewhere on the globe…

Jamaica · 1831

The Baptist War

The Baptist War begins in December 1831 as a strike led by Baptist deacon Samuel Sharpe and develops into the largest uprising by enslaved people in Jamaica. Tens of thousands participate or are affected, plantations are damaged across western parishes, and colonial repression is severe.
