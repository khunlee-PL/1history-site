Federated States of Micronesia · 1921

Japan's sugar empire on the reefs

Japan, granted the islands as a League mandate, turns them to profit. The Nan'yō Kōhatsu company clears land for sugar and ships in tens of thousands of Japanese and Okinawan settlers, until they outnumber the islanders in their own archipelago. Micronesians are pushed to the margins and conscripted for labor; the schools teach them Japanese and their place beneath it.

Meanwhile, elsewhere…

Rwanda · 1922

Ruanda-Urundi

Belgium administers Ruanda-Urundi under a League of Nations mandate from 1922 and governs Rwanda through the monarchy and chiefly hierarchy. Colonial racial theories and administrative policies harden Hutu-Tutsi distinctions and privilege Tutsi elites for much of the mandate period.

Mali · 1916

French Sudan sends tirailleurs to the Great War

France recruits and conscripts men from French Sudan into units commonly grouped under the tirailleurs sénégalais. Soldiers from the colony serve in France, the Mediterranean and other theaters during the First World War.

Across the world…

Zambia · 1911

Northern Rhodesia

Northern Rhodesia is created as a British South Africa Company-administered territory in 1911. Company rule prioritizes mining, taxation and labor recruitment before Britain assumes direct colonial administration in 1924.

In the same age…

Burkina Faso · 1932

Upper Volta is divided

France dissolves the colony of Upper Volta and divides its territory among Cote d'Ivoire, French Sudan and Niger. The Mossi heartland is governed from the coast for the next fifteen years.

And far away…

Mozambique · 1938

The forced cotton regime

Portuguese authorities require African farmers across northern Mozambique to grow cotton for sale to colonial concession companies at fixed prices. The system shapes rural life for decades and becomes a lasting grievance against colonial rule.

Elsewhere on the globe…

Belarus · 1921

The Peace of Riga splits Belarus

Poland and Soviet Russia end their war by drawing a border straight through the Belarusian lands. The west goes to Poland, the east to the Soviet republic. Belarusian-speaking territories are divided between Poland and the Soviet republic until 1939.
