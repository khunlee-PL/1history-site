Palestine · 565

The Church of the Nativity

At Bethlehem, the Church of the Nativity stands over the grotto venerated by Christians as the birthplace of Jesus. The present basilica largely dates to Justinian's sixth-century rebuilding and remains one of Christianity's oldest continuously used major churches.

Guatemala · 562

Tikal is broken

In 562 Tikal suffers a major defeat in the wars involving Calakmul and its allies. The defeat is followed by a long hiatus in dated monuments at Tikal, even though occupation continues; the gap is evidence of political disruption rather than proof that the city itself fell silent.

Mexico · 650

Teotihuacan burns

Around the sixth or seventh century, major elite and ceremonial buildings at Teotihuacan are burned and the city’s political system declines. Population falls sharply, but the causes remain debated and include internal conflict, environmental stress and wider regional change.

Scotland · 563

Columba lands on Iona

The Irish monk Columba founds a monastery on the tiny island of Iona, which becomes the springboard for the conversion of the Picts and a cradle of Insular Christianity.

Belize · 562

Caracol humbles Tikal

Caracol defeats Tikal in 562 during the shifting alliances and wars of the Classic Maya period. At its height the city controls a large territory supported by terraces and causeways; population estimates are substantial but vary widely.

And far away…

Kazakhstan · 568

Taraz, city of the Silk Road

Taraz grows on the Talas River as a market town where caravans trade between the steppe and the settled south. A Byzantine embassy to the Turks passes through the region in these years, noting its busy commerce.

Jordan · 560

The Madaba map

Craftsmen lay a mosaic map of the Holy Land in a church at Madaba, picturing Jerusalem and the Jordan in coloured stone. It survives as the oldest known map of the region.
