China · 2

The Han census of 2 CE

Han administrative records for 2 CE count roughly 12.2 million households and about 59.6 million registered people. The figures show the extraordinary reach of the imperial household-registration system, though they should not be read as a perfectly complete modern census.

Pakistan · 150

Greco-Buddhist art

Gandharan artists develop influential anthropomorphic images of the Buddha using artistic conventions shaped by South Asian, Iranian and Hellenistic traditions. Gandharan styles spread widely through Buddhist networks, alongside other important image-making centers such as Mathura.

North Macedonia · 69

Stobi on the Vardar

Where the Crna joins the Vardar, Stobi thrives as the chief town of the region. Its paved streets run past a theater, a Jewish synagogue later built over by a basilica, and floors of bright mosaic — peacocks and hunting scenes still in place. Sacked by Goths and shaken by quakes, it fades after the 6th century.

Vatican City · 64

Peter buried on the Hill

Christian tradition holds that the apostle Peter is crucified in or beside Nero's circus and buried close by, in a modest grave in the pagan-and-Christian cemetery on the slope. A red-plastered wall and a small shrine, the tropaion Gaius names around 200, mark the spot. The later basilicas are deliberately centered on the traditional site of Peter's tomb.

Armenia · 77

The temple at Garni

A colonnaded temple in Greco-Roman style stands above the Azat gorge at Garni, probably dating to the first century CE and associated with the reign of Tiridates I. Its precise original function remains debated, but it is the best-preserved classical temple in Armenia.

Wales · 78

The Roman fortress at Caerleon

Rome plants a great legionary fortress, Isca, on the Usk, complete with amphitheatre and baths, anchoring its grip on the Welsh tribes for generations.
