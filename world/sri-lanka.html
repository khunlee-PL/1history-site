Sri Lanka · 377 BCE

The founding of Anuradhapura

Anuradhapura becomes the capital of a kingdom that endures for more than a thousand years. It grows into one of South Asia's great early cities, built around irrigation tanks and Buddhist monasteries.

Meanwhile, elsewhere…

Dominica · 400 BCE

Saladoid canoes reach Waitukubuli

Arawakan-speaking Saladoid people paddle up the island chain from the Orinoco and settle the steep, well-watered island they will not name Dominica. They bring cassava, red-and-white painted pottery and village life to the river mouths of a land whose interior stays forest. Their word for it endures: Waitukubuli, 'tall is her body'.

Chad · 400 BCE

The Sao and their clay heads

South of the lake the Sao raise walled towns and mould striking terracotta heads and figures, some near life-size. Kotoko tradition remembers them as giants and claims their line; the Kotoko go on to build fortified brick city-states — Logone, Kousséri, Goulfey — along the Chari and Logone rivers.

Across the world…

Eritrea · 400 BCE

Qohaito on the plateau

High on a windswept plateau, the caravan town of Qohaito guards the road from the port at Adulis up to the interior. Stone pillars, a rock-cut dam and painted caves survive; traders rest here with ivory, obsidian and incense bound for the sea.

In the same age…

Kazakhstan · 400 BCE

The Golden Man of Issyk

In a kurgan on the Issyk River, a young Saka warrior is laid to rest in a suit covered with some four thousand gold plaques, now known as the Golden Man. The burial becomes one of the most celebrated finds of the Iron Age steppe.

And far away…

Liechtenstein · 400 BCE

Rhaetian people

Rhaetian tribes hold the upper Rhine, herding on terraces and trading metal over the passes. Their language, written in a north Italic alphabet on scattered inscriptions, is not Celtic and dies without an heir. What is now Liechtenstein is their high pasture, a corridor between the plains and the Alps rather than a place with a name of its own.

Yemen · 400 BCE

The Kingdom of Hadhramaut

The kingdom of Hadhramaut governs the frankincense country of south-eastern Arabia from its capital Shabwa. Its port of Qana ships incense across the Indian Ocean to markets as far as India.
