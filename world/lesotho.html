Lesotho · 1852

Battle of Berea

In 1852 British forces under Sir George Cathcart fight Basotho forces near the Berea Plateau after a dispute over cattle and colonial authority. The engagement is costly and inconclusive; Moshoeshoe's subsequent conciliatory letter helps end the campaign without further British advance.

Algeria · 1845

French conquest of Algeria

During the French conquest, troops under Colonel Aimable Pélissier seal members of the Ouled Riah inside caves in the Dahra and kill hundreds by smoke and fire. The wider conquest brings warfare, displacement, famine and disease and causes severe population loss.

Malawi · 1859

Livingstone reaches Lake Nyasa

On his Zambezi expedition the Scottish missionary and explorer David Livingstone travels up the Shire River and reaches the great lake he records as Lake Nyasa, publicising the region in Britain.

Across the world…

Grenada · 1843

Nutmeg makes the Isle of Spice

Nutmeg trees, carried from the East Indies, take to Grenada's volcanic soil. Within a lifetime the island becomes a major global nutmeg producer, the fruit later stamped on its flag. Split nutmeg and its red lace of mace still dry in the Gouyave sheds, the reason Grenada is called the Isle of Spice.

In the same age…

Botswana · 1852

The Boers attack at Dimawe

In 1852 a Transvaal commando attacks Sechele I's Bakwena at Dimawe during conflict over frontier authority, trade and the sheltering of refugees. The Bakwena resist successfully at the hill, but the attackers also raid settlements and carry away captives; the episode becomes an important confrontation between Tswana rulers and the South African Republic.

And far away…

Fiji · 1852

Cakobau, lord of Bau

Seru Epenisa Cakobau succeeds Tanoa as Vunivalu of Bau and later claims the title Tui Viti, or king of Fiji, though other chiefs do not universally accept his authority. His wars, alliances, conversion to Christianity and later role in cession make him a central figure in nineteenth-century Fijian politics.

Elsewhere on the globe…

Montenegro · 1852

Montenegro becomes a secular principality

Danilo, next in the line but unwilling to be ordained, refuses the bishop's mitre and has himself proclaimed prince. Rule passes from church to crown, from vladika to hereditary sovereign. Montenegro becomes a secular principality, still tiny and unrecognised, but now a monarchy that can be inherited like any other.
