El Salvador · 1524

Alvarado at Acajutla

In 1524 Pedro de Alvarado’s expedition meets a large Indigenous force near Acajutla. Alvarado reports being badly wounded in the leg during the fighting; Spanish forces hold the battlefield but face continued resistance as the conquest proceeds.

Mozambique · 1505

Portugal establishes rule on the Mozambique coast

Portugal establishes fortified positions on the Mozambique coast from the early sixteenth century and gradually expands influence along the Zambezi. The prazo estate system develops later in the interior, combining land grants, trade, armed power and coerced labor.

Columbus reaches the South American mainland

On his third voyage, Christopher Columbus reaches the Gulf of Paria and the Orinoco delta and steps ashore, his first landing on the South American mainland.

Mali · 1600

The Azalai salt caravan crosses the Sahara

The Azalai salt caravan sets out from Timbuktu across the Sahara to the mines of Taoudenni and back. Long files of camels carry grain and goods north and return laden with slabs of salt.

Guatemala · 1524

Alvarado attacks the K’iche’ kingdom

In 1524 Pedro de Alvarado leads Spanish and Indigenous allied forces into the Guatemalan highlands, defeating K’iche’ armies and executing K’iche’ leaders after entering Q’umarkaj. Conquest warfare, colonial labor systems, displacement and epidemic disease then drive severe population loss across the region.

And far away…

Honduras · 1524

Olid opens the conquest

In 1524 Cristóbal de Olid lands on the north coast to claim Honduras for Cortés, then breaks with his patron and tries to establish his own authority. Rival Spanish factions defeat and kill Olid before Cortés reaches Honduras, showing that the early conquest also involved violent competition among conquistadors.

Elsewhere on the globe…

Monaco · 1524

Monaco leased to Spain

Squeezed between France and the emperor, Lord Augustin Grimaldi places Monaco under the protection of Charles V of Spain. For over a century Spanish garrisons hold the Rock and Spanish subsidies pay for it, while the lords style themselves sovereign — an independence leased from a great power, the pattern of Monaco's whole survival.
