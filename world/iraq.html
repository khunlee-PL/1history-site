Iraq · 1800 BCE

Babylonian mathematics

A clay tablet lists Pythagorean triples a thousand years before Pythagoras, in base sixty — which is why an hour has sixty minutes.

Meanwhile, elsewhere…

Ghana · 1800 BCE

The Kintampo Complex

Farming and herding communities of the Kintampo Complex settle the woodlands of central Ghana, raising stone tools, polished rasps and daub houses. They are among the earliest food-producing societies known in the forest-savanna of West Africa.

At the same time…

Jordan · 1800 BCE

The town of Pella

A walled town rises at Pella in the Jordan Valley, trading with Egypt and the cities of the coast. Occupied across many ages, it becomes one of the cities of the later Decapolis.

Across the world…

Mauritania · 1800 BCE

The towns of Dhar Tichitt

As the wet Sahara dries, herders and grain farmers build hundreds of dry-stone villages along the Tichitt cliffs. Compounds of packed stone, granaries and cattle pens stand in rows, an early planned settlement in West Africa, raised by ancestors of the Soninke as the desert closes in around them.

In the same age…

North Macedonia · 1800 BCE

The Kokino archaeological site

Bronze Age communities use a volcanic ridge at Kokino for ritual activity. Some researchers interpret particular sightlines and markers as astronomical observations, and the site remains in use for centuries.

And far away…

Syria · 1750 BCE

Mari, Syria

The royal palace at Mari on the Euphrates preserves thousands of cuneiform tablets and letters from the early second millennium BCE. They reveal diplomacy, trade, court administration and the daily concerns of a kingdom connected across Mesopotamia and Syria.

Elsewhere on the globe…

Bahrain · 1900 BCE

Saar, Bahrain

Excavations at Saar reveal a planned Bronze Age settlement with houses, streets, seals and a central temple. Its material culture shows Dilmun's connections with wider Gulf and Mesopotamian trade networks.
