Cyprus · MÖ 1100

The city-kingdom of Salamis

Near the mouth of the Pedieos river, settlers found Salamis, which grows into the foremost of the island's city-kingdoms. Its harbor, markets, and later Roman gymnasium make it a center of Cypriot life for nearly two thousand years.

Syria · MÖ 1200

Late Bronze Age collapse

Ugarit is destroyed around the end of the Late Bronze Age, amid the wider collapse of eastern Mediterranean palace societies. Letters from the city's final decades describe military danger and disrupted shipping; alphabetic writing survives elsewhere after Ugarit's fall.

Belize · MÖ 1100

Cuello, the first villages

Cuello preserves early village occupation in the Maya lowlands, with houses, burials and pottery dating back well into the first millennium BCE and perhaps earlier. Its long sequence is important for understanding the emergence of settled farming communities in northern Belize.

Afghanistan · MÖ 1000

Zoroaster

Zoroaster, whose date and homeland remain debated, teaches a religion centered on Ahura Mazda, moral choice, and judgment after death. Zoroastrian ideas later developed in conversation with the religious worlds of Judaism, Christianity, and Islam, but direct lines of influence are debated.

Andorra · MÖ 1000

Madriu-Perafita-Claror Valley

In the glacial valley of Madriu-Perafita-Claror, herders drive sheep and cattle to the summer grass, raise dry-stone huts and smelt iron from the rock. Seasonal pastoralism and later ironworking help shape the cultural landscape that UNESCO now recognizes as World Heritage.

Central African Republic · MÖ 1000

Ubangian speakers take root

Ubangian languages have long been spoken across parts of the central African savanna. Archaeology and historical linguistics indicate deep population movements in the region, but modern Banda, Gbaya, Zande and Ngbaka identities cannot be traced to a single population at a precise date.
