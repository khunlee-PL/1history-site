Mongolia · 1206

The Mongol Empire

In 1206 Temüjin, having united the Mongol tribes, is proclaimed Genghis Khan, and launches the conquests his successors extend into history's largest contiguous land empire. Its expansion reshapes trade, warfare, and cultural exchange across Eurasia.

Antigua and Barbuda · 1300

Island Caribs

By the late pre-Columbian period, Kalinago-speaking communities are established across much of the Lesser Antilles, including Antigua. European accounts later emphasized warfare and raiding, but archaeology and ethnohistory also show regional travel, exchange, fishing and settlement.

Mauritania · 1300

Salt from the sebkha

Twice a year the camel caravans, the azalai, cross to the salt pans of Idjil, cutting bars of gray rock salt to trade south for millet and gold. Where the Sahel has no salt, a slab can weigh nearly against its own weight in gold.

Pakistan · 1206

Lahore under the sultans

Qutb al-Din Aibak, a former Ghurid commander who later founds the Mamluk dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate, uses Lahore as an important political center. The city remains strategically significant under several later dynasties, though its status varies greatly over time.

Armenia · 1205

The monastery of Noravank

In a narrow gorge of red cliffs, the monastery of Noravank is founded under the Orbelian princes. The sculptor Momik later carves its two-storey church, reached by a steep outer stair.

And far away…

Bangladesh · 1204

Bakhtiyar takes Bengal

Bakhtiyar Khalji conquers much of Bengal around the beginning of the thirteenth century. Later chronicles tell a dramatic story of his entering the Sena capital with only a small mounted party, but the details are uncertain; Muslim political power and Islamization then develop gradually over centuries.

Chad · 1210

Dunama Dabbalemi opens the mune

The mai Dunama Dabbalemi commands cavalry said to run into the thousands and pushes Kanem's reach from the Fezzan toward the Nile. He opens the mune, a sacred talisman of his ancestors, breaking a taboo — a rupture that tradition blames for the strife which follows.
