DR Congo · 1585

The Luba kingdom

East of the Upemba lakes, Kalala Ilunga's line builds a kingdom on a sacred kingship called bulopwe, blood that alone confers the right to rule. Its court, its titled offices and its lukasa memory-boards spread a model of statecraft across the southern savanna.

Mali · 1600

The Azalai salt caravan crosses the Sahara

The Azalai salt caravan sets out from Timbuktu across the Sahara to the mines of Taoudenni and back. Long files of camels carry grain and goods north and return laden with slabs of salt.

Mozambique · 1544

Portugal moves up the Zambezi

Portuguese traders and officials expand up the Zambezi during the sixteenth century, seeking control of gold, ivory and regional commerce. Over time the prazo system creates semi-autonomous estates whose power depends on local alliances, armed retainers and coerced labor.

Bolivia · 1650

Potosí

Potosí becomes one of the Spanish Empire's great silver-mining centers. From 1573 the colonial mita requires more than 200 Indigenous communities to send roughly one-seventh of their adult men for rotating labor service. Mining, mercury exposure, coercion and disease impose severe human costs, but later claims of eight million deaths are not a reliable demographic estimate.

Barbados · 1660

Sugar plantations in the Caribbean

Barbadian sugar estates combine wind- or animal-powered mills with boiling houses where cane juice is processed through heated copper vessels. Enslaved workers face long hours, heat, machinery and other serious hazards throughout cane cultivation and sugar processing.

Venezuela · 1498

Columbus reaches the South American mainland

On his third voyage, Christopher Columbus reaches the Gulf of Paria and the Orinoco delta and steps ashore, his first landing on the South American mainland.

Cabo Verde · 1585

Drake burns Ribeira Grande

Francis Drake lands a thousand men and takes Ribeira Grande without a fight; the townsfolk have fled inland. His fleet holds the empty town for two weeks, demands a ransom that never comes, then burns it before sailing on for the Caribbean. The sack exposes how defenseless the rich slaving port has become.
